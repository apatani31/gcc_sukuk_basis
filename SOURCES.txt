1. PRELIMINARY RESEARCH (literature used in Research Brief 1)
======================================================================

Umar, Z., Abrar, A., Hadhri, S., & Sokolova, T. (2023). The connectedness of
    oil shocks, green bonds, sukuks and conventional bonds. Energy Economics,
    119, 106562. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eneco.2023.106562
    Use: oil supply/demand/risk shocks and index-level spillovers into sukuk.
    Note: confirm the shock decomposition and index names against the full
    text (Penn Libraries).

Saeed, M., Elnahass, M., Izzeldin, M., & Tsionas, M. (2021). Yield spread
    determinants of sukuk and conventional bonds. Economic Modelling, 105,
    105664. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.econmod.2021.105664
    Use: firm-level spread determinants, 58 listed Malaysian firms; stale-
    pricing caveat for sukuk.

Fitch Ratings. (2026, February). Sukuk and bond pricing correlation study,
    2025 data. As reported in Saxena, A. (2026, February 5). Prices of sukuk
    and bond maintain solid correlation. Gulf Daily News.
    https://www.gdnonline.com/Details/1375249/Prices-of-sukuk-and-bond-maintain-solid-correlation
    Use: same-issuer sukuk/bond pairs (54 pairs, 0.97 correlation, sukuk
    about 6bp tighter). Cite the Fitch report directly if obtained
    (fitchratings.com, free with registration).

3. METHODS (econometric techniques used in the pipeline)
======================================================================

Newey, W. K., & West, K. D. (1987). A simple, positive semi-definite,
    heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent covariance matrix.
    Econometrica, 55(3), 703-708. https://doi.org/10.2307/1913610
    Use: HAC standard errors for issuer time series and event-study tests.

Driscoll, J. C., & Kraay, A. C. (1998). Consistent covariance matrix
    estimation with spatially dependent panel data. Review of Economics and
    Statistics, 80(4), 549-560. https://doi.org/10.1162/003465398557825
    Use: panel standard errors robust to common monthly shocks.

Cameron, A. C., Gelbach, J. B., & Miller, D. L. (2008). Bootstrap-based
    improvements for inference with clustered errors. Review of Economics and
    Statistics, 90(3), 414-427. https://doi.org/10.1162/rest.90.3.414
    Use: wild cluster bootstrap with few clusters (six CDS governments).

Webb, M. D. (2023). Reworking wild bootstrap-based inference for clustered
    errors. Canadian Journal of Economics, 56(3), 839-858.
    https://doi.org/10.1111/caje.12661
    Use: six-point (Webb) weights for the wild cluster bootstrap.

Holm, S. (1979). A simple sequentially rejective multiple test procedure.
    Scandinavian Journal of Statistics, 6(2), 65-70.
    Use: multiple-testing correction across the mechanism tests (task 8).
